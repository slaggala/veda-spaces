# Suggested ownership structure for the 54 visible promises

**Status: suggestion only.** No person is named and nothing is confirmed. Operations assigns real people and records
each confirmation in the promise matrix through a reviewed pull request. Until then all 54 promises stay BLOCKED and
UNASSIGNED, and activation is refused.

## Structure

- **One accountable function per promise**, from the five groups below. Each group has a named **accountable lead** and a
  named **backup**, and they must be different people. In a small team one person may lead several groups, but nobody
  should be both the lead and the backup of the same promise.
- **Verification partners:** the checks in each promise's chain cross functions (for example a Procurement promise is
  checked on site by Site Execution). The partner signs its own check; the accountable lead confirms the whole row.
- **Owner (founder):** approves wording (approval package, Part 1) and activation (Part 2). The owner is not the
  accountable lead of the operational checks, so that every promise has an operational owner who can actually verify it.
- **Promise review:** a monthly 30-minute review of the matrix by the five leads. Any promise found not to be kept is
  re-opened (status BLOCKED) at once. That automatically blocks reactivation until it is fixed and re-confirmed.
- **Exception path:** substitutions and deviations go through the quotation revision approved by the designer and
  accepted by the customer before procurement (every row's exception process).

| Group | Suggested accountable lead | Suggested backup | Promises | Why this group |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Procurement** | Procurement lead | A project manager | 13 | The promise is a bought-in brand, grade or product. Procurement owns the PO, supplier and receipt checks; Site Execution verifies before fitting. |
| **Projects** | Head of projects (senior project manager) | Another project manager | 6 | The promise is about what a project delivers (room scope, typical sizes, the delivery process). The project manager owns quotation-to-handover delivery. |
| **Site Execution** | Site execution lead (senior site supervisor) | A project manager | 3 | The package text and components are activities on site (protection, freight, debris, cleaning, pest-control preparation), and installation is done on site. |
| **Customer Support** | Customer support lead | Sales head (for callbacks); a project manager (for warranty documents) | 6 | Warranty, after-handover service, consent and the callback and consultation commitments are kept by customer-facing support. |
| **Sales** | Sales head | A senior designer | 26 | Estimate wording, package and allowance explanations, exclusions and client scope, and design-stage selections are what sales says and quotes. |

## Notes

- `rooms_note` ("Room amounts … include installation") needs both a sales and an operations answer. It sits with
  Site Execution because installation is carried out there.
- `approved_examples` and the material categories depend on sales decisions S1–S8. Procurement confirms only what
  sales has approved.
- `callback` depends on sales decision S10. If the one-working-day commitment is dropped, the row's wording changes
  first.
