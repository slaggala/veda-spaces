| Proposed behavior | Layered limits. Known and unknown identifiers behave identically; no tier locks an account (except the certified 05 §4 lock for accounts without a factor), but a victim CAN be temporarily disrupted, as stated below. (1) Network limits: 10/min per client IP (IPv6 per /64) and 30/min per wide network (IPv4 /24, IPv6 /48) on login; 5/min and 20/min on forgot; 10/min per IP on MFA verify, MFA recovery, enroll confirm, email verify and email cancel. (2) Identifier-per-network limits: 5/min login and 3/hour forgot per (HMAC of the normalized email, /24 or /64), so a third party cannot rate-limit the victim from every network (IR-23). (3) Account budget across all networks: 10 password failures in a sliding 15 minutes → every attempt for that identifier needs a solved Turnstile challenge, from any network; 20 → attempts are also spaced by an escalating interval (2 s doubling to 30 s; 429 + Retry-After). The correct password is not evaluated without the challenge (closes the MFA_REQUIRED oracle). A successful sign-in does not reset the budget. (4) Aggregate: 200 failures across all identifiers in 5 minutes → challenge for every sign-in while the window holds. (5) MFA: 10 MFA, recovery or enrollment-confirmation failures per account in 15 minutes → MFA throttled 15 minutes (05 §4). (6) Reset emails: at most 3 per account per hour across all networks; further requests get the identical 202 and send nothing. (7) The certified per-(account, network) throttle and 5-network global lock for accounts without a factor are unchanged. Trusted proxy: CF-Connecting-IP is believed only from configured peers, each at most /24 or /64; otherwise the TCP peer is the client. Privacy: limiter keys and events carry an HMAC of the normalized email, never the address. Cardinality: in-process tables capped at 50 000 keys each (expired entries evicted first, then the oldest); one application process (OPS-010). |
| Reason | Keep IR-23 (no victim lock-out from a third party's network) while restoring a cross-network bound on password guessing for every account, including MFA holders, and on reset-email volume. |
| Implementation (provenance) | Implemented at 6ec2e76 (unchanged at 3f5920b): api/veda/platform/auth/throttle.py (account budget, aggregate, reset cap, bounded tables), api/veda/kernel/ratelimit.py (wide-network and hashed identifier keys), api/veda/platform/auth/service.py (login and forgot), api/veda/platform/auth/routes.py (endpoint limits), api/veda/config.py (trusted-proxy prefix bound). |
| Security impact | Not claimed: per-account throttling alone does not bound password guessing (the certified lock exempts factor holders); the bound comes from tiers (1)–(4) together. Distributed guessing per account: ≤ 10 unchallenged attempts per 15 minutes, then one solved challenge per attempt, then ≤ 1 attempt per 30 s; an attacker with a human or paid challenge-solving budget can still guess slowly. Victim disruption that remains possible: (a) FC-05 — a third party can send the three reset emails early in an hour; each new link invalidates the previous one and links expire after 30 minutes, so the victim can be without a usable reset link for roughly half of every hour while the attack continues (the victim can still sign in with the password; an administrator can issue a reset); (b) while an identifier is escalated the victim must solve a challenge and may wait up to 30 s per attempt. Turnstile/provider outage (FC-A06): escalation lasts as long as failures keep arriving (sliding 15-minute window), and a challenge cannot be verified during an outage, so an escalated identifier cannot sign in for the whole outage-under-attack plus up to 15 minutes after failures stop. This is fail-closed and not bounded by 15 minutes under a sustained attack. Identifiers that are not escalated are unaffected; aggregate escalation during an outage refuses every password sign-in until the aggregate window drains. Accuracy (FC-A05): the budget check and record are separate steps, so concurrent in-flight attempts can exceed a threshold by the number of requests in flight. |
